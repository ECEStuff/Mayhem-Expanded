May the 4th be with you! The mod has been updated to 1.14. 

Highlights
- Shield recycling and loot return.
- Improved pop growth and migration.
- Reworked hacking mechanics and fortress perk.
- Nerfed NPC fleet scaling to improve performance in larger galaxies.
- Updated TIS.

Previous savegames compatibility: YES*. 
Previous galaxies compatibility: YES.

* This update is compatible with existing saves. However, starting a new game is recommended to fully benefit from the reworked NPC scaling and improved performance in large galaxies.

		ADDITIONS
-----------------------------------------
- Shield recycling and loot return
  - On hard and insane, ships retain up to 75% and 50% of shields.
  - When the player recycles a ship, its shields and lasers are now added to the Outpost.
  - Looters no longer destroy shield containers. They will now pick them up.
  - Excess shields can now be deposited when a ship drops its loot (looter or drop wares command).
  - To encourage their reuse, shields recycle for 0.5% (0.4% on insane) of their value.
  - Rationale: 
    - Captured ships may be missing shields, and the player uses more captured ships. NPCs and pirates do not have this issue.
    - Unlike lasers, shields are not sold by NPC trading stations or pirate bases. This is to encourage the player to salvage shields rather than buy them.

- Pop growth, assembly, and migration
  - Pop growth no longer uses current population to determine growth. It now uses the effective population (current + reserved). This is the same principle used in pop assembly since 1.12.1.
  - Added a helper script which calculates the number of reserved pops (population cost to build ships)
  - Changed pop growth script to return 3 items: final natural growth, maximum natural growth, and growth percentage.
  - Outpost GUI now displays population growth percentage and number of reserved pops.
  - Emigration no longer has a 33% chance to kill 1 emigrant. It will now always transfer 5 pops per cycle or fewer if the destination lacks space.
    - Fixed emigration not respecting adjusted max cap. Now uses the restricted cap rather than absolute cap.

- Hacking
  - Restored AFS availability for smaller ships (was restricted to bigger ships in 1.13 and 1.13.1).
  - Added accounting counterhack:
    - For OCV playthroughs, player loses red crystals.
    - For normal players, player loses credits. Credit counterhack was previously unused.
  - Reworked counterhacking:
    - Counterhacking now hacks additional ships if there aren't enough preferred ships.
      - It attempts to hack 2 more ships of any type if it wants to hack big ships (M6, TM, M8, capships) and 4 more if it wants to hack capships.
      - For example, if counterhacking wants to hack 2 of the player's capships but the player has only 1 capship, then it will hack 4 more ships of any type - including fighters, TS, M6, TM, M8, and TP.
    - Counterhacks are now performed in parallel rather than sequentially. This means that up to N number of ships will be counterhacked at once rather than one at a time.
    - Targeted systems are now cached rather than determined on the spot.
    - Replaced oxygen counterhack with weapons and cargo counterhack with accounting.
    - Adjusted counterhack probabilities as follows:
      - Shields: 67% -> 50%
      - Weapons (previously oxygen): 11% -> 25%
      - Hull damage: 11% -> 15%
      - Accounting (previously cargo): 11% -> 10%
  - Changed hacking effects:
    - Accounting: payout is now equal to the ship's weighted relvalue * hack efficiency.
      - Weighted relvalue is the relvalue of the ship in most cases. However, M7, M1, and M2 relvalues are divided to smoothen scaling.
      - Removed OCV playthrough penalty.
    - Navigation: Reduced duration by 50%: 100 seconds per 1% efficiency -> 50 seconds per 1% efficiency.
  - Changed Navigation, Shields, and Weapons hack: additional hacks on the same ship now extend their timer.
    - The player will receive a notification that the hack duration was extended.
  - Reduced port patch time (time needed before you can hack the same ship again if its ports are patched) from 60-90 hours to 30-42 hours.
  - When dispatching a virus from a hacking station, a maximum of 40 random relays are now shown rather than a full list. 
    - Prior to this, assembling the full list was performance heavy due to checking each sector and its ships within virus range.

  - Rationale: 
    - Makes hacking more strategic and riskier. A duration of 60-90 hours to patch is effectively 2-4 days, and the 4-hour random ship update is too slow if the player is hacking a lot of ships.
    - Parity between OCV and normal playthroughs, along with player and NPCs. 
    - Getting hacked feels bad. However, savescumming allows the player to avoid the nastier hacks. 
      - Previously, only the shields and hull hack had meaningful damage. O2 protected the player's bailed ships from NPC tugs, and cargo hack was negated if the ship had no cargo or if the player immediately ordered the affected ship to collect its dropped cargo.
      - Now, all the counterhacks are meaningful, meaning the player has to suffer a debuff in all counterhacking cases. 
      - Determining the systems long before forces the player to either accept the consequences or invest in defenses: AFS and/or the hacking defense program.
    - Accounting hack was changed so that the payout is appropriate for the hacked ship class.
    - Navigation hack was nerfed due to how long it could effectively disable a ship compared to shields and weapons. The adjusted duration is still long enough that even a low efficiency hack will disable the ship for a couple sectors worth of travel.
      - This hack is not in the counterhack list because the player can instantly cure ships by issuing commands.

- Fortress perk
  - Destroying the Outpost will now destroy all lasertowers and militia fighters.
  - Increased minimum number of militia fighters: 2 -> 4
  - Capital shipyard now properly doubles the number of militia fighters. Previously, only the maximum cap was doubled. Now it doubles both the minimum and maximum.
  - Militia fighters now use the player's blueprints. If the player lacks valid blueprints of a specific class (M3 or M4), then militia fighters default to the player's origin race (and Yaki if a non-Xenon Defect player has infamy).
    - A blueprint is considered valid if:
      - It is not hidden (see Personal Console -> Blueprints menu).
      - Its research is complete.
      - It is not an OCV ship.
      - It is an M3 or an M4. 
  - Militia fighters no longer cost maintenance and supply, but they can no longer be recycled.
  - Reduced lasertower respawn timer from 20 hours to 8 hours.
  - Adjusted lasertower initial spawns and cap:
    - Player: 8 -> 10
    - NPC: 24 -> 20

  - Rationale: 
    - The 8 hour respawn time lines up with the 8 hour faction update timer. Every 8 hours, factions may get the Fortress perk. 
    - As such, having the respawn timer set to 8 hours ensures synchronization. As for initial spawns, this lines up with the cap: no Outpost should start with fewer or more lasertowers than the cap.
    - Militia fighters were buffed for consistency due to the reduced number of lasertowers that the player can have at once.
    - Free supply and maintenance because militia fighters are local to the Outpost and not to the player's fleet. However, because these are free ships, they cannot be recycled (prevents exploits).

- NPC and OCV fleet scaling
   - Overextension factor now decays when the player is no longer above the supply cap.
    - Maximum decay rate per hour increased: 10% -> 25%
  - Tall needs now have reduced weight to prevent excessive fleet generation in large galaxies.
  - Raiders now get 1 additional fleet per 75 sectors rather than 1 per 50 sectors.
  - NPC scaling in response to player's overextension is now dependent on galaxy size.
    - Fewer than 86 sectors: up to 400%
    - 86-144 sectors: up to 300%
    - 145+ sectors: up to 200%
  - Rationale: 
    - Large galaxies naturally generate more sectors, stations, and population. 
    - Previously all of these scaled fleet demand additively, leading to exponential growth in active fleets. 
    - Tall scaling has been softened to keep difficulty while preventing entity bloat, SETA slowdown, and FPS drops. 
    - The scaling adjustment reduces initial fleet spawns by approximately 20%, as tested in large galaxies.

- Terracorp Interorbital Storage (TIS)
  - TIS dropship is now treated as protected Terracorp logistics.
    - The TIS dropship now spawns with full internal defenses (AFS, HPD, ISL), preventing hacking and significantly increasing boarding difficulty.
    - Destroying or boarding the TIS dropship now incurs heavy infamy penalties (+20 / +35) and temporarily suspends TIS access.
  - TIS now removes empty wares from its list.
  - Rationale:
    - Without maintaining TIS, empty wares cluttered the list. This ensures only stored wares are shown.
    - The consequences also discourage boarding it in an attempt to get "free" items while it's loading wares.

  - Mitigated a potential race condition with bailing.
    - Root cause: the bailing operation isn't atomic, and Mayhem.Task.Bail.Copy is inherently non-atomic as it's a global task script. 
      - In theory, the best approach requires eliminating the START [NULL] keywords before calling Bail.Copy. However, this has a tradeoff with leaving ghost ships for a couple seconds. 
      - However, Bail.Copy CANNOT run on the source ship; otherwise, the script will be interrupted by !ship.signal.killed during CopyShip.
    - The second best approach was ultimately taken. It makes !ship.signal.killed authoritative and blocks external contenders (other scripts attempting to bail the source at the same time, particularly when hacking is involved).
