* 5.0 - Mordan9


. Add new Player "Capital Shipyard".

	The Capital Shipyard sports improved statistics across the board.  These stats do not vary based on game difficulty, only the cost of the upgrade.
	
	* 9 capital docking slots
	* 2 Additional Perks
	* 50% additional Tax Income
	* 20% increase to base Recycling effeciency
	* 25% boost to Ship Production speed.


	The Shipyard cannot be directly purchased or built.  They are an upgrade to an existing deployed Outpost.  The upgrade activation
	is found in your outpost Factory menu near the bottom.	The cost for this upgrade will be listed there and will vary depending
	on game difficulty.  This is a VERY expensive upgrade.  Upgrade cost varies with game difficulty level.
	
	Once activated, the existing outpost will be converted to a capital shipyard capable of docking 9 capital sized ships at the docking ports.
	The existing outpost will be destroyed for Scrap, so remove everything you want to keep.  Any ships owned by the outpost will be orphaned.
	Population, along with both ship and station specialization levels WILL be preserved through the transition.
	Note:  If you transfer such a sector to the A.I. they will only get a standard outpost in the exchange.

.  Extended Agent Range
	You can now extend your outpost Agent range beyond the default while in-game.
	Player Outpost Agents and Outpost Traders have a Default range of 8 jumps, which is set the the 9973 t-file.
	Now, if you increase the Trade Range of an Outpost using the "Station Parameters" screen, the Agents and Traders 
	working there will observe the new extended range.  Care should be taken to limit going above 8 jumps, as the Agent algorithms
	for seeking trades are CPU intensive, and increasing that range from 8 to 12 results in a very large search area.
	If you have performance issues, you can change the t-file setting back down to 4 (the old game default), and then
	if you need more range for a given outpost, just bump up that single Outposts' trade range setting.

.  Satellite Removal
	Added a process where the A.I. justifyably treats your satellites as Spy Satellites and will remove some of them from their space
	until you become better friends.  The same is true for A.I. satellites in player owned sectors.
	When this setting is enabled/active in the 9973 t-file, there is a calculated chance every 20 minutes that a player satellite in A.I
	space will be removed, and a chance that any A.I. satellites in the Player's space will be removed.  
	Game difficulty level, and player notoriety and infamy determine the chance your satelites will be removed.
	No notoriety changes or hostilities will occur due to the removal of your satellites.	Once you achieve Notoriety level 6 (3333 notoriety) 
	with a race, your satellites in their space will be unaffected.	This setting defaults to 1 (ON) and can be turned on in the 9973 t-file.

.  Increased all OOS ship damages.
	At some point during development of Zero Hour, it was determined that OOS (player out of system) battles should occur very very
	slowly to give the player more time to react to fights and bring in reinforcements, or respond directly.  While this was helpful, 
	it was a very overpowered alteration that can be easily exploited.  
	
	The main problem caused by doing this is that it allows the player to 'game' the system by moving In-System for battles where
	they are strong, allowing them to destroy a lot of assets very quickly, giving the A.I. vastly less time to react, when that very 
	same ability simply isn't an option to your A.I. opponent.  It allows the Player to rapidly speed up battles in systems of their
	choosing, where THEY are strong, while leaving battles in systems where they are weak to play out very slowly allowing the player
	more time to reenforce.
	
	In Z.H., Weapon re-firing delays were set to roughly 5x slower when OOS, and Weapons damage amounts were roughly 6x less when OOS.	
	In the 4.0 release of Renegades, I did some minor reduction of the weapons fire delays from 5x down to 3x.  
	In this release, I have changed the Weapons damage reduction for OOS battles down from 6x to roughly 3x.  
	This will help most battles resolve faster, and further limit the players ability to exploit the tactical advantages this gives.

.  Added the Raider NPC Fleet type.
	This is a new raiding fleet that only the A.I. commonwealth nations possess.  Composed of roughly a dozen M3/M4 class fast fighters, this 
	type of fleet will perform gorilla style War-time deep anti-logistics operations against other hostile nations, including the Player.  This fleet type
	is not considered to be a "Battle Group" and does not observe the "Deep strike" option found in the 9972-L044.xml file.  This fleet
	WILL penetrate your space during war time and kill TS Class transports if it gets the opportunity.  On hard difficulty level, the A.I.
	will have an increased chance to go after the player rather than other A.I. nations.
	
	Each Commonwealth nation will possess such a fleet and will use it to greatly weaken whomever they are at war with from a logistics standpoint.  
	Left alone, these fleets can decimate outpost agents, traders, and other needed logistics ships within other empires, including your own.
	From one perspective, this can be viewed as a bonus to the Player, as you will likely be far more responsive to such a threat than the 
	A.I. is, and the Commonwealth nations will seriously hurt each other from time to time.  If you are not prepared though, it can put
	a large hurt on your ass.
	
	It is recommended, when at war, for the player to keep at least one M3/M4 fleet available to respond to threats within your 
	sectors.  The A.I. will weigh the mobile defenses of a sector against the value of the juicy TS targets there and will continue to prosecute
	the targets there until a response is mounted, it runs out of TS ships to kill, or it has lost sufficient fleet strength to continue.
	The A.I. raider fleet will move from sector to sector killing as it goes, until something is done to stop it.
